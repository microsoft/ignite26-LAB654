Top Business Priorities for Negotiation

I've organized these in priority order. These are the hills we need to be prepared to fight on.

1. Ownership of Fine-Tuned Model Weights (Vendor Portability)

This is the #1 business priority, full stop. Our proprietary loss-history and actuarial data are being used to fine-tune the Trey Research base models. Zava must own — or at an absolute minimum co-own — the fine-tuned model weights so that if the vendor relationship ends, we can port those models to another vendor or bring them in-house. All outputs and inferences generated from Zava data must be our sole property. Trey Research should not be permitted to use our data for cross-customer model improvement without our express opt-in consent.

I'm flagging this as non-negotiable. Without IP portability, we are locked into this vendor regardless of what other transition rights exist on paper. The transition rights discussion (Priority #4) is meaningless if we can't take our models with us.

2. Robust Model Change-Control to Protect Underwriting Accuracy

Any changes to the AI models that affect underwriting outcomes need to be subject to rigorous change-control. Material changes should be defined to include any change resulting in greater than 2% variance in output distribution or any change to training data sources. We need a meaningful advance notice period — not just days — and prior written approval rights for material changes.

Luc is especially concerned about model deprecation disrupting live underwriting pipelines. If a model version is deprecated, Zava needs continued access to the deprecated version for a substantial period so underwriting operations aren't disrupted while we validate the replacement. Model cards should be delivered with each model version so we can assess changes before they hit production.

3. Regulatory-Grade Explainability for State Insurance Examiners

We need contractual commitments to explainability standards aligned with the NIST AI RMF "Measure" function. Specifically, this must include bias testing on a quarterly cadence, annual independent third-party algorithmic audits, and data-lineage and training-data provenance representations. State examiners in Colorado and New York may demand this information, and we cannot be in a position where the vendor refuses to cooperate with regulatory inquiries or withholds documentation we need to satisfy an examination.

4. Strong Transition Rights to Avoid Vendor Lock-In

If the relationship ends for any reason, we need a meaningful transition period — not just a few months of grudging cooperation. Transition services should be available for a substantial period at rates that don't constitute economic coercion. Data return must be in industry-standard, portable formats (Apache Parquet or CSV), not proprietary formats, and must be completed within 30 calendar days of termination notice.

Source-code escrow should be established with Iron Mountain Intellectual Property Management, Inc. (consistent with our Playbook requirements) so that if Trey Research becomes insolvent, we have access to the code. This ties directly back to Priority #1 — IP portability and transition rights together are how we avoid lock-in.

5. Adequate Liability Protection

Given the regulated nature of the use case and the volume — 4 million decisions per month flowing through production underwriting — liability caps need to be commensurate with the actual risk. A liability cap pegged to a fraction of annual fees would be wholly inadequate. We need caps tied to a meaningful multiple of ACV.

Indemnification must cover not just patent claims but also copyright, trade secret, and critically, AI-specific output liability — including infringement and defamation arising from AI outputs. On the insurance side, we should be requiring CGL, E&O/Tech PI, cyber liability, and an AI-specific E&O rider, all at levels appropriate for a $14.75M deal in a regulated sector.
